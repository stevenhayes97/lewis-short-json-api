calliditas | 1 | cunning
calliditas | 3 | in the bad sense, craft and slyness: scientia remota ab justitia calliditas potius quam sapientia, knowledge parted from justice deserves the name of cunning rather than wisdom
calliditas | 4 | in the good sense, shrewdness or skill: calliditas ingenii, quickness of wit
calliditas | 5 | the bad sense is much the commoner and the more classical
callidus | 1 | shrewd
callidus | 2 | cunning
callidus | 3 | made wise by practice, expert and adroit; Cicero explains it from callum, a mind grown hard with use
callidus | 4 | with a genitive, skilled in something: callidus rei militaris, versed in soldiering
callidus | 5 | in the Greek manner with an infinitive: callidus condere furto, clever at hiding by theft
callidus | 6 | of things, well contrived: callidissimo artificio fabricata, made with the most cunning craft
callidus | 7 | in the bad sense, crafty and sly: callidum consilium, a sly plan; callida juris interpretatio, a crafty reading of the law
callidus | 8 | as an adverb callide, in both senses alike, cleverly or craftily
